Thesis

Whitfield is winning, and the stack under it is new: the group says Whitfield finished a new e-shop engine and mobile app in 2025, and online is the brand's fastest-growing channel. Nobody should walk in selling a Whitfield replatform. The tension sits next door. In January Calder closed the Marlowe deal, and Marlowe arrived with its own Magento estate, its own customer base and a much higher online share. The group's Q2 report draws the line in writing: Marlowe keeps full separation in identity, product, communication and its offline and online sales network, while cooperation targets "areas invisible to the end customer", naming online sales development, logistics, IT and data analytics. Two brands, two stacks, one segment number, and a stated plan to share the back end. The three angles:

  • The back end is now the merger. The report separates the customer-facing layer from the invisible layer and puts online, IT and data in the second. That is a systems decision with no public owner yet (revenue, cost) [F1]. Discovery question: which parts of Marlowe's online now sit with him?
  • The smaller brand sells online better. Marlowe's online share in Q2 runs at more than twice Whitfield's first-half share, while mall traffic falls and online carries the growth. The know-how may flow from Marlowe to Whitfield, not only the other way (revenue) [F4][F5][F6]. Discovery question: what does Marlowe do online that Whitfield does not?
  • Online growth carries a cost line management already named. Jewelry selling costs grew faster than jewelry revenue, and the report blames couriers, packaging and preparing online parcels, in a group whose CEO calls gross margin the most important goal (cost) [F1][F3]. Discovery question: what does an online order cost to fulfill today, per brand?

The problem, in their words: "Marlowe has to stay fully separate for the customer, but everything the customer never sees, online sales, logistics, IT and data, we now run twice." An insider should nod, not squint [H].
